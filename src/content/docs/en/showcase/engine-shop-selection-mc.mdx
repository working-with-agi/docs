<Aside type="tip" title="Part 2">
  This page is part 2 of [Engine MRO Optimization](/en/showcase/engine-mro-optimization/). Part 1 decides *when* and *with which workscope* deterministically; this page decides **which shop** to send each engine to, under uncertainty in cost overruns, TAT delays, FX and unscheduled removals.

When sending engine shop visits to external shops, comparing the quoted prices alone does not tell you where to send them. Who pays for extra work driven by teardown findings? How much can the TAT slip? For foreign-currency quotes, how will the exchange rate move? If another engine fails in the meantime, are there enough spares? On top of that come company constraints: fiscal-year budgets, LLP kits on hand, minimum-volume contracts with shops. This showcase solves all of this as a **two-stage stochastic MILP over Monte Carlo scenarios** and decides at once:

- the **shop, workscope, induction month and whether to expedite** for each planned shop visit
- the number of **spare engines to lease long-term** for the whole horizon

It then answers the first question planners actually get asked: **"Which engines do we have to decide on now?"**

<Aside type="caution" title="About the data">
  All quotes, risk profiles, fleet data and budgets on this page are **synthetic and illustrative**. They are not quotes from any OEM or real shop. Results depend heavily on the assumptions (spare ratio, lease availability in peak season, etc.); calibrate with your own data before use.
</Aside>

## 1. The problem: a quote is "price + risk distribution"

For the same workscope, shops differ in contract type and risk profile.

| Shop (sample) | Contract | `overrun_share` | Currency | Characteristics |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| `IN-HOUSE` in-house shop | T&M (time and material) | 1.0 | Home | No shipping, 1 slot, booked 1 month ahead. The operator pays all findings overruns. Expedite available |
| `OEM-NET` OEM network shop | Fixed price | 0.0 | USD | Higher quote but no overrun and small delays. 1 month shipping, 2 slots, booked 4 months ahead. Expedite available. Minimum-volume contract of 2 visits |
| `IND-MRO` independent MRO | Capped T&M (50% share) | 0.5 | USD | Cheapest quote but longer TAT and widely spread congestion delays. Booked 2 months ahead |

`overrun_share` is the share of findings-driven overrun borne by the operator, so it captures the contract type in one number: 1 for T&M, 0 for fixed price, somewhere in between for capped T&M or shared overruns. Price differences between quotes only become comparable once this share, the delay distributions and FX risk are taken into account.

Moreover, per-shop decisions are not independent:

- If delays hit several shops in the same months that visits overlap, spares run short and aircraft go **AOG** (aircraft on ground, i.e. disruptions). In peak season the loss is even larger.
- **Unscheduled removals** during that period (bird strike, FOD, borescope findings) make the shortage worse.
- Whether to secure spares up front with long-term leases or react with short-term leases must be decided together with shop selection.
- Fiscal-year budgets, LLP kits on hand and minimum-volume contracts limit which combinations are allowed.

### Common random numbers

Whether teardown findings exist is a property of the engine's condition and should not depend on which shop it goes to. So within a scenario, **every shop uses the same random draws** for a given engine (common random numbers). Shops differ only in how likely they are to bill the findings (`findings.prob`), the overrun distribution, and who pays (`overrun_share`). This keeps shop-to-shop differences from being buried in sampling noise. Congestion delay (`delay.shop_*`) is shared by all engines at the same shop within a scenario, and a single FX path per scenario is shared by all foreign-currency quotes.

## 2. Should we hurry? (decision deadlines)

Before choosing a plan, the first practical question is **"which engines must we decide on now?"** For a given plan, `shop_mc/urgency.py` computes the following for each engine:

```
decision deadline = planned induction month − longest lead time

  lead-time candidates
    shop slot booking   booking_lead_months of the chosen shop
    LLP kit order       llp_kits.lead_time_months, only if the workscope uses an LLP kit
                        and the kits on hand are already committed to earlier visits
                        (kits on hand are allocated in order of induction month)

status
  deadline ≤ 0 months away  → "decide this month"
  deadline ≤ 3 months away  → "within 3 months"
  otherwise                 → "can wait"
```

The **cost-of-timing curve** moves only that engine to each month of its removal window (other engines fixed), re-chooses shop, workscope and expedite among the options available in that month to minimize expected total cost, and evaluates the result. Options that break the shop-slot, LLP-kit or fiscal-year-budget limits are excluded; if no option remains in a month, the curve records **which constraint blocked it** ("shop slots", "LLP kits", "FY2027 budget", etc.).

```
hurry cost = expected total cost at the earliest feasible month on the curve − plan's expected total cost
wait cost  = expected total cost at the latest feasible month on the curve   − plan's expected total cost
```

Curves are evaluated on a common scenario sample (2,000 by default, `--urgency-scenarios`).

In the sample, for the stochastic plan, E-901 and E-902 are "decide this month", and E-904 (4-month OEM slot booking) and E-906 (8-month LLP kit order) are "within 3 months". Engines whose deadline is further away can wait for updated quotes or teardown results before being decided.

## 4. Mathematical model: two-stage stochastic MILP

This section mirrors the formulation in `shop_mc/model.py`. Money is in k$, time in months.

### First and second stage

```
First stage (decided now, before uncertainty is revealed)
  x[i] ∈ {0,1}            adopt option i = (engine e, shop k, workscope w, induction month t, expedite r)
                           t within the removal window; w allowed and quoted by shop k;
                           r = expedite only if the shop offers it
  S    ∈ {0,…,S^max}      long-term spare engines leased for the whole horizon
  q[k] ≥ 0                shortfall against shop k's contracted volume

Second stage (per scenario s and month t, after costs / delays / removals are known)
  l[s,t] ∈ [0, cap_t]     short-term lease engines (cap_t = max_engines_peak in peak months)
  a[s,t] ≥ 0              uncovered positions (AOG); cost c_aog,t carries the peak multiplier
```

Writing `x[e,k,w,t]` below means the same thing as `x[i]`. The sample has 490 options (196 of them expedited).

### Scenario-defined parameters

Each Monte Carlo scenario s fixes:

```
cost[s,i]   shop visit cost borne by the operator for option i
            = ( price[k,w] · (1 + overrun_share[k] · ρ[s,e,k]) + rush_fee[k]·[r_i] ) · FX[s]^[k foreign-currency]
              + transport_cost[k]
            ρ[s,e,k] = overrun ratio if findings are billed (lognormal), else 0
            FX[s]    = FX multiplier (mean 1, lognormal σ = fx.volatility), shared by all foreign quotes
down[s,i]   off-wing months of option i
            = transport_months[k] + TAT_i + shop_delay[s,k] + engine_delay[s,e,k]
            TAT_i = tat[k,w], or max(1, tat[k,w] − tat_reduction[k]) if expedited
A[s,i,t]    = 1  if  t_i ≤ t < t_i + down[s,i]      (off-wing in month t)
U[s,t]      engines off-wing from unscheduled removals (Poisson arrivals × duration distribution)
```

### First-stage fixed cost

```
F_i = green_time_value · (latest_e − t_i)   −   V[w_i]
      life thrown away by removing early        on-wing life the workscope builds
```

Removing an engine before its limit `latest` throws away that many months of on-wing life. `V[w]` (`workscope_build_value`) gives deeper workscopes larger credits; without it, a 24-month view would always pick the cheapest PR.

### Constraints

```
(1) each planned visit happens exactly once
    Σ_{i ∈ I(e)} x[i] = 1                                                ∀ e

(2) planned shop capacity (quoted TAT, reduced if expedited)
    Σ_{i : k_i = k, t_i ≤ t < t_i + TAT_i} x[i] ≤ slots[k]              ∀ k, t

(3) LLP kits: only the kits on hand can be used before new orders arrive
    Σ_{i : w_i ∈ LLP WS, t_i < lead_LLP} x[i] ≤ kits_on_hand

(4) fiscal-year budget (expected spend booked in the induction month's fiscal year)
    Σ_{i : FY(t_i) = y} Ē[cost_i] · x[i] ≤ budget_y                     ∀ y
    Ē[cost_i] = mean of cost[s,i] over the scenario set being optimized

(5) take-or-pay volume commitments
    q[k] ≥ min_visits_k − Σ_{i : k_i = k} x[i]                          ∀ k with a commitment

(6) position coverage (per scenario and month)
    owned − Σ_i A[s,i,t] · x[i] − U[s,t] + S + l[s,t] + a[s,t] ≥ D      ∀ s, t
```

(2) is a **planning** constraint that reserves shop slots by quoted TAT, excluding shipping and delays. The actual overlaps caused by delays are priced through (6) as spare shortfall (lease and AOG cost). Because (4) constrains an expectation, its relationship to the budget depends on which scenario set a method optimizes over (this is why "pessimistic P90" turns out infeasible below).

### Objective (risk-neutral)

```
min  c_S · T · S  +  Σ_i F_i · x[i]  +  Σ_k pen_k · q[k]                  first stage
     + (1/N) Σ_s Q_s                                                     sample average of second stage

     Q_s = Σ_i cost[s,i] · x[i]  +  Σ_t ( c_l · l[s,t] + c_aog,t · a[s,t] )
     c_aog,t = aog_cost_per_month · aog_peak_multiplier[calendar month(t)]
```

### CVaR option

With `--cvar-weight λ` (λ > 0), the objective becomes the expectation plus CVaR of the **total cost**:

```
min  E[total cost] + λ · CVaR_α[total cost]

total cost_s = (first stage: c_S·T·S + Σ_i F_i·x[i] + Σ_k pen_k·q[k])  +  Q_s
```

CVaR uses the Rockafellar–Uryasev linearization:

```
min  (risk-neutral objective above)  +  λ · ( η + 1/((1−α)N) · Σ_s z[s] )
s.t. z[s] ≥ first stage + Q_s − η,   z[s] ≥ 0                          ∀ s
     (η free = estimate of VaR_α, α = --cvar-alpha, default 0.9)
```

The first-stage terms are identical in every scenario, but including them inside the CVaR also weights them by λ. The model therefore optimizes the same "CVaR of total cost" that the out-of-sample CVaR90 measures.

### Problem size (sample data)

490 first-stage options (binary) plus integer `S` and shortfall `q`. With N = 200 scenarios over 24 months, the second stage has 9,600 continuous variables and 4,800 coverage constraints.

## 5. Computational pipeline

```
1. Monte Carlo          sample N scenarios (overruns, delays, FX, unscheduled removals)  scenarios.sample
2. SAA                  solve the two-stage stochastic MILP (default N = 200)            model.solve_saa
3. EV plan              solve the mean-value deterministic problem (benchmark)           scenarios.mean_value
4. Out-of-sample eval   evaluate both plans on independent scenarios (default 5,000)     evaluate.evaluate
                        → mean, P50/P90, CVaR90, disruption probability, VSS = EV − SAA
5. Gap estimate         optimality gap from M independent SAA lower bounds and           evaluate.saa_gap
                        the out-of-sample value (upper bound)
6. Deadlines            decision deadlines and cost-of-timing curves per plan            urgency.analyse
```

The **EV plan** solves a single deterministic scenario built from the average of 20,000 scenarios (off-wing months rounded to whole months, unscheduled removals as a fractional steady-state average). It corresponds to the traditional "plan with averages" approach; its gap to the SAA plan is the **VSS** (Value of the Stochastic Solution).

For **out-of-sample evaluation**, once the first stage (x, S) is fixed the second stage has a closed form:

```
shortfall[s,t] = max(0, D − (owned − Σ_i A[s,i,t] x[i] − U[s,t] + S))
l[s,t]         = min(shortfall[s,t], cap_t)
a[s,t]         = shortfall[s,t] − l[s,t]
total_s        = first stage (long-term spares, F_i, volume shortfall penalties) + Σ_i cost[s,i] x[i]
                 + Σ_t ( c_l · l[s,t] + c_aog,t · a[s,t] )
```

Evaluation needs no MILP, only NumPy vector operations, so thousands of scenarios are fast. Disruption probability is the share of scenarios in which `a[s,t] > 0` in at least one month.

The **optimality gap** follows Mak–Morton–Wood:

```
lower  L̄ = (1/M) Σ_m v̂_m        v̂_m: optimal value of independent SAA problem m (solver dual bound)
upper  Ū = out-of-sample mean cost of the candidate plan
gap = Ū − L̄,   one-sided 95% upper limit = gap + 1.645 · sqrt( s_L² / M + s_U² )
```

The expected SAA optimal value is no greater than the true optimum (downward bias), so `L̄` is a statistical lower bound, and the candidate's out-of-sample value is an unbiased upper bound. Using the solver's dual bound for each SAA keeps the lower bound valid even if a MIP is not solved to optimality. The estimate is valid for the risk-neutral objective only and is skipped when `--cvar-weight` is set.

### Monte Carlo is not "the solver"

Here, Monte Carlo is used for **scenario generation** and **plan evaluation**. The MILP solver is what searches for the plan.

- "Generate random candidate plans, simulate each, keep the best" cannot meaningfully search the combinations of 490 options and spare counts, and guarantees neither budget / LLP-kit feasibility nor optimality.
- SAA uses a MILP to optimize **one plan** that must perform across all scenarios at once. Tail correlations, such as overlapping visits coinciding with delays, act directly through constraint (6).
- Evaluating on the same scenarios used for optimization is optimistically biased (optimization bias). So evaluation uses **independent** scenarios, and the gap estimate checks whether the scenario count is sufficient.

The three deterministic models are each solved on a single scenario:

- **Quotes at face value**: quoted price and TAT as stated; findings overrun, delays and unscheduled removals all set to zero
- **Expected value (EV)**: the mean over 20,000 scenarios
- **Pessimistic P90**: the 90th percentile over 20,000 scenarios (off-wing months rounded up)

What the results show:

- Taking quotes at face value gives about 2.5 times the disruption probability of the stochastic plan. Quotes must be treated as "price + risk distribution".
- With every cost at its P90, no plan satisfies the fiscal-year budgets, LLP kits and shop slots at the same time. This signals that the budget is sufficient only "on average".
- Under the budget constraint, the stochastic plan pushes the FULL visit into the new fiscal year (April) and assigns the 2 LLP kits on hand to the early inductions.
- Strengthening CVaR raises the mean by about 1,000 k$, lowers disruption probability from 8.0% to 5.3%, and lowers P90 and CVaR90 as well.
- The sample's assumptions (about 10% spares, 2 lease engines available in peak season, etc.) drive the results strongly. Calibrate with real data before use.

  The figures above depend strongly on synthetic values such as the spare ratio, AOG cost, peak multipliers and findings probabilities; both the level of disruption probability and the ranking of methods can change with the assumptions. For real use, calibrate the inputs with your own reliability data, invoice history and budgets, and check that the assumptions are plausible in the input-distribution panel described below.

## 7. Comparing methods in parallel and choosing (`compare.py`)

`compare.py` solves the 7 methods in the table above (quotes at face value, expected value, pessimistic P90, risk-neutral SAA, CVaR λ = 0.3 / 1 / 3) **in a process pool**, evaluates every plan on **the same evaluation sample** (5,000 scenarios by default), and runs the deadline analysis for each feasible plan in parallel as well (a few tens of seconds with 4 workers on the sample).

```bash
python compare.py                  # solve 7 methods in parallel; write compare.json and compare.html
python compare.py --workers 8
```

| Option | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `--workers N` | CPU cores | Parallel workers |
| `--scenarios N` / `--eval-scenarios N` | `200` / `5000` | SAA scenarios / common evaluation scenarios |
| `--urgency-scenarios N` | `2000` | Scenarios for the cost-of-timing curves |
| `--time-limit SEC` | `300` | MILP time limit per method [s] |
| `--json-out` / `--html-out` | `compare.json` / `compare.html` | Output paths |
| `--fragment` | — | Emit an HTML fragment without `<html>`/`<head>` |

```
          ┌── Quotes at face value ──┐
          ├── Expected value (EV) ───┤
          ├── Pessimistic P90 ───────┤   ProcessPoolExecutor
 data ────┼── SAA risk-neutral ──────┼── (one HiGHS thread per worker)
          ├── CVaR λ=0.3 ────────────┤   infeasible methods are recorded as such
          ├── CVaR λ=1 ──────────────┤
          └── CVaR λ=3 ──────────────┘
                       │
                       ▼
   evaluate all plans on one common sample of 5,000 scenarios (closed-form recourse)
                       │
                       ▼
   deadline analysis per plan (parallel) → Pareto set → compare.json + compare.html
```

### Pareto set

Feasible plans are compared on three metrics, all minimized: **mean cost, disruption probability and CVaR90**. A plan is Pareto-optimal if no other plan is at least as good on all three and strictly better on at least one. Because every plan is evaluated on the same scenarios, differences in the metrics reflect differences between the plans themselves. Methods that produce identical plans (e.g. λ=1 and λ=3) are flagged as such.

### The chooser (`compare.html`)

A single self-contained HTML file with no external dependencies, laid out top to bottom as "should we hurry?", "which plan?", "does it fit the company's constraints?" and "are the assumed uncertainties plausible?":

1. **Should we hurry?** — for the selected plan: the number of engines to decide this month, each engine's decision deadline (and the binding lead time), the cost of hurrying or waiting, and the cost-of-timing curve per induction month (peak months shaded; months blocked by a constraint marked × with the constraint's name)
2. **Which plan?** — a "Keep cost down ↔ Avoid disruptions" slider (with "cost first", "balanced" and "avoid disruptions first" presets), a cost × disruption-probability scatter plot with the Pareto front, and the selected plan's total-cost distribution (histogram against the EV plan)
3. **Company constraints** — budget consumption per fiscal year (expected spend ÷ budget), LLP kits (used within the lead time / on hand, and kits to order), minimum-volume achievement and penalties, on-wing life thrown away by early removal (months and value), and expedite count; plus the induction schedule (colored by shop, expected off-wing time and range up to P90, with peak months and fiscal-year boundaries)
4. **Input distributions** — next section
5. **Comparison table of all methods** — infeasible methods are shown too

The slider's recommendation is computed as follows, where the weight w ∈ [0, 1] is the slider position (0 at the left, 1 at the right):

```
norm(v) = (v − min) / (max − min)        min, max over all feasible candidates

score(m) = (1 − w) · norm(mean cost_m) + w · norm(disruption probability_m)

recommendation = argmin score(m)   over Pareto-optimal plans m only
```

Mean cost (k$) and disruption probability (%) have different units, so each is normalized to its relative position among the candidates before weighting. A plan that is not Pareto-optimal is never recommended, whatever the weight. The planner can also pick a plan directly from the table or charts; moving the slider returns to the recommendation.

### Input distribution panel

This panel shows 20,000 draws from the same random model used for the scenarios, so planners can see that quotes are treated as "price plus this distribution" rather than a single price (`scenarios.input_distributions`).

| Panel | Content |

| **Unscheduled removals** | Distribution of the fleet-wide count over 24 months (Poisson from MTBUR); each one takes an engine away for 2–4 months |
| **Seasonality of AOG cost** | AOG cost per month (with peak multipliers) and the short-term lease cap in each month |

<Aside type="tip" title="How this fits the SDK">
  With the WorkWithAGI SDK, the intended use is: an agent runs `compare.py` inside the sandbox created by `createSession` to generate the HTML, and the application shows the chooser on the planner's screen. `--fragment` outputs a fragment without `<html>`/`<head>`, which is easier to embed in an existing page. The agent lays out and evaluates the candidate methods; **when to decide and which plan to adopt remain the planner's call**.
</Aside>

## 8. How to run

<Steps>

1. Install dependencies (`pulp`, `highspy`, `numpy`).

   ```bash
   cd examples/engine-shop-selection-mc
   pip install -r requirements.txt
   ```

2. Compare the risk-neutral SAA plan (200 scenarios) with the EV plan.

   ```bash
   python run.py
   ```

3. Add gap estimation, risk aversion or your own data as needed.

   ```bash
   python run.py --replications 5                  # with statistical optimality gap
   python run.py --cvar-weight 1.0                 # risk-averse
   python run.py --shops my_quotes.json --json-out plan.json
   python -m unittest discover -s tests
   ```

4. Generate the parallel comparison of 7 methods and the chooser.

   ```bash
   python compare.py --workers 4
   ```

</Steps>

`run.py` options:

| Option | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `--fleet PATH` | `data/fleet_visits.json` | Fleet, planned visits and company constraints |
| `--shops PATH` | `data/shop_quotes.json` | Shop quotes and risk profiles |
| `--scenarios N` | `200` | Scenarios used by SAA |
| `--eval-scenarios N` | `5000` | Out-of-sample evaluation scenarios |
| `--cvar-weight λ` | `0` | Risk-aversion weight (0 = risk-neutral) |
| `--cvar-alpha α` | `0.9` | CVaR confidence level |
| `--replications M` | `0` | SAA replications for the gap estimate (effective at 2 or more) |
| `--seed` | `42` | Random seed |
| `--threads N` | — | HiGHS parallel threads |
| `--time-limit SEC` | `120` | Time limit per MILP [s] |
| `--json-out PATH` | — | Save metrics, the SAA plan (shop, workscope, month, expedite) and long-term spare count as JSON |

If no plan satisfies the budgets, LLP kits and shop slots at the same time, `solve_saa` raises `InfeasibleError` (with a message pointing at budgets, LLP kits on hand and shop slots). `compare.py` keeps such a method in the table as "infeasible".

## 9. Extensions

| Extension | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Connect to part 1** | Feed the integrated plan from [part 1](/en/showcase/engine-mro-optimization/) into `window` / `allowed_workscopes` / `workscope_build_value`, deciding "when and what" and "where" hierarchically |
| **Multi-stage (rolling) re-optimization** | Re-optimize remaining visits as information arrives, e.g. firm quotes after teardown inspection; engines whose deadline lies ahead can be decided in these later runs |
| **L-shaped method (Benders decomposition)** | Split the second stage into per-scenario subproblems to scale the scenario count; also helps tail estimation for CVaR |
| **Uncertain shop slots and shipping** | Make shop capacity itself uncertain (congestion from other customers) and model shipping lead time as a distribution |
